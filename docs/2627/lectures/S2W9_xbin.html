what are \(b_0\) and \(b_1\)?

The Model:
\[ \color{red}{\text{ProblemSolvingScore}} = \color{blue}{b_0 + b_1 \cdot (\underbrace{\text{Condition="Normal"}}_{\text{1 if 'Normal'}\\\text{0 otherwise}})} + \color{black}{\varepsilon} \]

  • for people in the “Interrupted” group:

\[ \begin{align} \color{red}{\text{ProblemSolvingScore}} &= \color{blue}{b_0 + b_1 \cdot 0} \\ &= \color{blue}{b_0} \\ \end{align} \]

  • for people in the “Normal” group:

\[ \begin{align} \color{red}{\text{ProblemSolvingScore}} &= \color{blue}{b_0 + b_1 \cdot 1} \\ &= \color{blue}{b_0 + b_1 } \end{align} \]

what are \(b_0\) and \(b_1\)?

The Model:
\[ \color{red}{\text{ProblemSolvingScore}} = \color{blue}{b_0 + b_1 \cdot \text{(Condition="Normal")}} + \color{black}{\varepsilon} \]

Write up

We fit a simple linear regression model to estimate whether the sleep condition (“Interrupted” vs “Normal”, treatment coded with “Normal” as the reference level) would predict scores on a problem solving task. On average, people in the “Normal” sleep condition scored 57.5 on the problem solving task (\(b=57.50, SE = 0.94, t(78) = 61.35, p<.001\)). Compared to those in the Normal sleep condition, those with interrupted sleep scored on average 7.5 points lower (\(b = -7.50, SE = 1.58, t(78)=-4.73, p<.001\)), with a 95% confidence interval of [-10.65, -4.35].

common tests as linear model

lm() with continuous predictor

\[ \color{red}{\text{Y}} = \color{blue}{b_0 + b_1 \cdot \text{X}} + \color{black}{\varepsilon} \]

  • \(\color{blue}{b_0}\) = mean Y when X=0
  • \(\color{blue}{b_1}\) = change in mean Y from X=x to X=x+1

lm() with binary predictor

\[ \color{red}{\text{Y}} = \color{blue}{b_0 + b_1 \cdot \text{(X="categoryB")}} + \color{black}{\varepsilon} \]

  • \(\color{blue}{b_0}\) = mean Y when X is “categoryA”
  • \(\color{blue}{b_1}\) = change in mean Y from when X is “categoryA” to when X is “categoryB”

back to correlations

All of this is “how does Y change as X changes?”

We started whole discussion of linear models with scatterplots and correlations.

Correlation is a standardised metric for characterising the linear association

linear model = “How much does Y change as X increases by 1?”


correlation = “How many SDs does Y change as X increases by 1 SD?”
