what are \(b_0\) and \(b_1\)?

The Model:
\[ \color{red}{\text{ProblemSolvingScore}} = \color{blue}{b_0 + b_1 \cdot (\underbrace{\text{SleepQuality="Good"}}_{\text{1 if 'Good'}\\\text{0 otherwise}})} + \color{black}{\varepsilon} \]

  • for people with “Bad” sleep quality:

\[ \begin{align} \color{red}{\text{ProblemSolvingScore}} &= \color{blue}{b_0 + b_1 \cdot 0} \\ &= \color{blue}{b_0} \\ \end{align} \]

  • for people with “Good” sleep quality:

\[ \begin{align} \color{red}{\text{ProblemSolvingScore}} &= \color{blue}{b_0 + b_1 \cdot 1} \\ &= \color{blue}{b_0 + b_1 } \end{align} \]

what are \(b_0\) and \(b_1\)?

The Model:
\[ \color{red}{\text{ProblemSolvingScore}} = \color{blue}{b_0 + b_1 \cdot \text{(SleepQuality="Good")}} + \color{black}{\varepsilon} \]

Write up

We fit a simple linear regression model to estimate whether the quality of sleep (“Bad” vs “Good”, treatment coded with “Good” as the reference level) would predict scores on a problem solving task. On average, people reporting “Good” sleep scored 50 on the problem solving task (\(b=57.50, SE = 1.14, t(58) = 50.39, p<.001\)). Compared to those who reported “Good” sleep, those who reported “Bad” sleep scored on average 7.5 points lower (\(b = -7.50, SE = 1.88, t(58)=-3.98, p<.001\)), with a 95% confidence interval of [-11.27, -3.73].

common tests as linear model

lm() with continuous predictor

\[ \color{red}{\text{Y}} = \color{blue}{b_0 + b_1 \cdot \text{X}} + \color{black}{\varepsilon} \]

  • \(\color{blue}{b_0}\) = mean Y when X=0
  • \(\color{blue}{b_1}\) = change in mean Y from X=x to X=x+1

lm() with binary predictor

\[ \color{red}{\text{Y}} = \color{blue}{b_0 + b_1 \cdot \text{(X="categoryB")}} + \color{black}{\varepsilon} \]

  • \(\color{blue}{b_0}\) = mean Y when X is “categoryA”
  • \(\color{blue}{b_1}\) = change in mean Y from when X is “categoryA” to when X is “categoryB”
