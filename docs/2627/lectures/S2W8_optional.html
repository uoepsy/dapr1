degrees of freedom?

  • We have 80 datapoints.

  • We’ve used 2 things (an intercept and a slope across X) to explain some of the variance in Y

  • The leftover unexplained variance in Y is created by \(n-2\) things.

  • why \(n-2\)?

  • we can place \(n-2\) of the datapoints absolutely anywhere, and still get the same line by fixing the last 2 datapoints.

    • think “give me 5 numbers that add up to 100” — once you’ve chosen the first 4 numbers, you have no free choice for the last one!

  • this idea is referred to as “degrees of freedom”

Write up

We fit a simple linear regression model to estimate whether the number of nights of disrupted sleep would predict scores on a problem solving task. The model was statistically significant, F(1, 48) = 7.6, p = .007, accounting for 9% of the variance in problem solving scores (\(R^2=0.09\)). More sleep disruption significantly predicted poorer problem solving (\(b = -2.0, SE = 0.73, t(78)=-2.76, p=.007\)), with a 95% confidence interval of [-3.45, -0.55], indicating that for every additional night of disrupted sleep, scores on the puzzle solving task decreased on average by 2 points.
