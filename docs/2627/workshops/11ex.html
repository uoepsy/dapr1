You will be working with the DAPR1 class survey data again, available at https://uoepsy.github.io/data/dapr1_2627_survey.csv. Load the data into R before answering any questions.

This week, you will focus on the procrast variable, which measures how often students tend to procrastinate on tasks or responsibilities on a scale from 0 (never) to 100 (always). The neutral midpoint of this scale is 50, which represents “about half the time”.

Our research question for this workshop (using a 5% significance level) is:

Do university students report procrastinating significantly more than the neutral midpoint of 50?

Question 1

Once you have created the plot, write a few sentences that summarise what you observe. Does the bulk of the distribution appear to sit above or below 50?

Question 2

Based on our research question, specify the null (\(H_0\)) and alternative (\(H_1\)) hypotheses.

Provide your answer in both plain English and mathematical symbols (using \(\mu\) for the population mean). In your dissertation or research you only need one of the two, but we want you to practice both this week.

TipHints

Remember that the null hypothesis always states “no difference”, “no effect”, or “nothing interesting going on”. The alternative hypothesis should reflect the direction of our research question.

  • Null hypothesis (\(H_0\)): The mean procrastination score of university students does not differ from the neutral midpoint of 50.
    In symbols: \(H_0: \mu = 50\)

  • Alternative hypothesis (\(H_1\)): The mean procrastination score of university students is greater than the neutral midpoint of 50.
    In symbols: \(H_1: \mu > 50\)

Question 3

Use the t.test() function in R to test your hypotheses.

TipHints
  • The R code is t.test([data]$[variable], mu = [value], alternative = "[direction]", conf.level = [value]).
  • The value specified in \(H_0\) goes in mu = [value].
  • Alternative can either be "two.sided", "less", "greater" and it corresponds to the direction of \(H_1\).
  • A 95% confidence level corresponds to an alpha of .05.

t.test(sdata$procrast, mu = 50, alternative = "greater", conf.level = 0.95)

Question 5

Let’s now practice how to report the results of a one-sample t-test using APA style.

First, calculate the sample mean and standard deviation of the procrast variable using the summarise() function.

Then, write up your one-sample t-test results. Your write-up should include:

  • A explanation of what was tested.
  • The sample mean (\(M\)) and standard deviation (\(SD\)).
  • The test results in APA format: \(t\)([degrees of freedom]) = [t-statistic], \(p\) = [p-value], [one-tailed/two-tailed].
  • A final sentence to explain whether the null hypothesis was rejected and what this means in context.
TipHints

This is the write-up from the lecture slides as an example:

We wanted to find out whether college students score above the threshold for “low loneliness” on the UCLA Loneliness scale, which is 34. At the 5% significance level, we performed a one-sided test of significance against the null hypothesis that college students’ loneliness is equal to 34. The results indicate that the mean loneliness for college students, 40.3, is significantly higher than the “low loneliness” threshold of 34 (\(t\)(486) = 15, \(p\) < .001, one-tailed).

sdata |> 
  summarise(
    M = mean(procrast, na.rm = TRUE),
    SD = sd(procrast, na.rm = TRUE)
  )
# A tibble: 1 × 2
      M    SD
  <dbl> <dbl>
1  60.6  25.5

A one-sample t-test was conducted to determine whether university students report procrastinating at significantly higher levels than the 50 midpoint. The results indicated that the mean procrastination score (\(M\) = 60.61, \(SD\) = 25.49) was significantly greater than 50, \(t\)(132) = 4.8, \(p\) < .001, one-tailed. Therefore, we reject the null hypothesis and conclude that students, on average, report procrastinating more than “about half the time”.
